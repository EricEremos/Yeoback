The shared lesson is not that one chart is universally best. Parent scope, units and semantics must be explicit, and the user's question determines which visual earns its space.

## Transfer case 1: backup restore planner

**New decision:** choose an older file version when restoring it may replace a newer local version. This task was not one of the 150 storage-style studies.

**Application:** F03 distinguishes the current local version, selected backup version and predicted replacement result. F08 favors an aligned comparison with a persistent path and a side-by-side version inspector. F04 keeps the replacement scope and recovery limitations visible before the action. F11 preserves missing backup-integrity evidence as unknown. A neutral material from F02 could support this structure without implying verification.

**Counterexample:** a green “Restore 2 GB” tile and reassuring character suggest the older copy is verified and all files can be recovered. The amount says nothing about version correctness or backup integrity.

**Correction:** display the exact path, dates, version differences, integrity status and replacement consequence; require the selected restore action after review. Distinguish a predicted result from the observed result after completion.

**Self-check:** current/selected/predicted states, task-based comparison, visible consequence and a corrected failure case are explicitly present in the proposed specification. A prototype and participant task are still needed to establish comprehension.

## Transfer case 2: cloud-cost dashboard

**New decision:** decide whether to change resource usage when month-end cost is uncertain.

**Application:** F03 separates billed-to-date measurement, forecast range, budget target and a conditional savings scenario. F06 uses a common time/amount scale with exact values and an uncertainty explanation. F05 prevents a red treatment from inventing urgency; F11 prevents projected savings from being called achieved savings. F04 separates reviewing a recommendation from shutting down a resource.

**Counterexample:** a glowing progress ring says “Saved $400” after merely recommending that an instance be stopped. It conflates recommendation, action and result, and hides the forecast's uncertainty.

**Correction:** say “Estimated monthly reduction if this resource is stopped,” specify the assumptions and potential service impact, and show the observed billing result only when available. Keep the stop operation separate from the cost explanation.

**Self-check:** observation, forecast and budget are distinct; the chart answers a defined comparison; the service consequence stays visible; the misleading ring claim is corrected. Forecast accuracy and usability remain untested.

## Transfer case 3: photo-library duplicate review

**New decision:** choose between similar images that may differ in resolution, edits or personal value.

**Application:** F11 distinguishes byte-identical duplicates from visual similarity. F08 supports side-by-side image comparison with persistent metadata; F04 separates grouping from selecting and deleting. F10 prevents repeated thumbnails from implying a precise count of recoverable duplicates. F05 avoids gamifying the number discarded.

**Counterexample:** a cute broom groups three similar portraits and automatically selects two as unnecessary. Similarity alone cannot establish which expression, edit or resolution the user values.

**Correction:** label the match type and its limits, show resolution/edit status and paths, keep ambiguous images unselected, and preview the exact removal selection. Show reclaimed space only after observing the operation's result.

**Self-check:** observed match evidence and inferred redundancy are separate; side-by-side review matches the choice; removal consequences and uncertainty remain visible; an unsafe inference is corrected. Actual matching quality and user preference are not evaluated here.

## What was learned, and what was not proved

The exercise produced reusable distinctions, detected rationale/render mismatches, and applied the rules to three new problem descriptions. That is evidence of explicit synthesis and application in this task. It is not evidence of long-term model memory, participant benefit, a blind transfer score or universal design superiority.

The next meaningful validation would put a chosen structure into a working prototype and test concrete comprehension and review tasks. Repeating the same self-check without a new artifact or independent observation would not strengthen this evidence.
